How items are chosen

  1. Harvest. Scholarly metadata (OpenAlex), the World Bank documents database, a global news index (GDELT, restricted to the outlets listed below), publisher and institutional RSS feeds, watched publication pages, and alert emails (Bloomberg, Google Scholar, Google Alerts). Only titles, links, dates and abstracts are collected.
  2. De-duplicate. Preprint, repository and journal versions of the same work are merged, keeping the journal version. Syndicated copies of a news story are clustered, keeping the best outlet. Anything shown in an earlier week is not shown again.
  3. Gate. News and research must mention a finance term and either a case-study city or institution plus a theme term, or two theme terms. Reports need one city or theme term. Titles with negative keywords are dropped.
  4. Venue check. Journals are graded by OpenAlex h-index. Watched journals are trusted, deny-listed venues are dropped, and low-signal venues are flagged and never pre-selected.
  5. Annotate. Claude rates relevance (0–3) against the project brief and scholarly substance (0–2), assigns cities and themes, and drafts a one-line note.
  6. Review. A team member ticks what to publish. News is only pre-selected at relevance 3.
  7. From the archive. Older works (10+ years) cited by this week's new research.
